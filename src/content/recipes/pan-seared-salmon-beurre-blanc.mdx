Pan-seared salmon with beurre blanc is one of those dishes that defines restaurant cooking—and one that's absolutely achievable at home. The combination of perfectly crispy salmon skin and silky French butter sauce is pure elegance.

## The Two Techniques That Matter

This dish is really about mastering two things: crispy skin salmon and classic beurre blanc. Let me break them down.

### Crispy Skin Salmon

The secret isn't complicated, but it requires patience:

**Dry the fish thoroughly.** Moisture is the enemy of crispy skin. Pat your salmon with paper towels multiple times until there's no moisture left on the surface.

**Start with a screaming hot pan.** A cold pan means the skin will stick and steam. Heat your pan for at least 2-3 minutes before adding oil.

**Don't touch it.** Once the salmon hits the pan, leave it alone. Pressing with a spatula for the first 30 seconds helps with contact, but after that, patience is key.

### Beurre Blanc: The Classic French Butter Sauce

Beurre blanc ("white butter") sounds fancy, but it's essentially an emulsion—cold butter whisked into a warm acid reduction. The key is temperature: too hot and the butter melts into grease; too cold and it won't emulsify.

The cream in this recipe acts as a stabilizer, making the sauce more forgiving. It's not strictly traditional, but it's what I recommend for home cooks making this for the first time.

## Why I Love This Dish

This is the dish I make when I want to impress someone without spending hours in the kitchen. Once you understand the techniques, it comes together in 30 minutes. The components can be prepped ahead: salmon dried and seasoned, butter cubed and chilled, shallots minced.

For a complete meal, serve alongside some simply steamed asparagus or a light salad. The richness of the sauce means you want something bright and fresh to balance it.

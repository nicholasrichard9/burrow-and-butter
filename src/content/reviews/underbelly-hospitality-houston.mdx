As a chef, I'll admit I was skeptical of "upscale BBQ." The best brisket in Texas typically comes from pits behind gas stations, cooked by pitmasters who'd laugh at a wine list. But Georgia James made me a believer.

## What Sets It Apart

Georgia James comes from Chris Shepherd's Underbelly Hospitality group, and it shows. This isn't a pitmaster's passion project—it's a chef-driven restaurant that happens to focus on smoked meats. That means thoughtful sourcing (44 Farms beef, heritage pork), a real bar program, and service that doesn't involve standing in line for two hours.

But here's the thing: despite the fine dining trappings, the BBQ fundamentals are rock solid. The brisket comes from a proper offset smoker burning post oak. The bark is developed naturally, not rushed. The smoke ring is deep and genuine.

## The Brisket

Let me be direct: this is some of the best brisket I've eaten anywhere in Texas, and I don't say that lightly.

The fat cap is perfectly rendered—that's harder than it sounds with prime brisket, which has more marbling to begin with. The bark is aggressive but not bitter. The meat pulls apart with the slightest pressure while still holding together. And the smoke flavor is clean oak, not acrid or overpowering.

Is it worth $32 a pound? For special occasion BBQ in a sit-down restaurant with full service? Absolutely.

## The Beef Ribs

If the brisket is perfect, the beef ribs are absurd—in the best way. These are massive dinosaur bones coated in mahogany bark, with meat that jiggles like jello when you shake the bone.

One rib could feed two people, maybe three. The fat renders completely, basting the meat from within. This is BBQ at its most primal and satisfying.

## The One Miss

The potato salad was fine but forgettable. In a menu this strong, "fine" stands out as the weak link. It's a minor complaint—this isn't what you come here for anyway.

## The Verdict

Georgia James proves you can elevate BBQ without compromising what makes it great. The sourcing is impeccable, the technique is flawless, and the restaurant experience adds genuine value over standing in line at a traditional joint.

Is it expensive? Yes. Is it worth it for a celebration or a special night out? Without question. This is the BBQ restaurant I bring visiting chefs to when they ask about Houston's food scene.

A perfect 5 out of 5.
